# The team that writes a customer message

Read this before dispatching. One agent alone wrote three rejected e-mails, and its specialist version with sources was
rejected too. What worked was opposing lines and readers who owed the text nothing.

## The brief, on disk

One file, read by everyone: who reads and what they remember, what the human wants in their words, the facts that may
be used, the locks, and a table `rejected text → their reason`. It grows on every rejection, so no round repeats a dead move.

## The round

1. **Two writers on opposite lines**, for example "the offer is the news" against "the founder talks straight". Each
   delivers one finished piece plus two alternative openings, and says the thread and what was cut.
2. **Two critics who see only the brief and the texts.**
   - A reader in persona: reads the real voice research first, says where they stopped, what sounds false, the word they
     would never use, whether they click. They are told not to be kind.
   - An adversarial editor: judges the thread, cuts sentences, checks every claim against the owner docs, and answers
     with `old → new`, never a rewrite.
3. **The lead merges.** The reader's stop line and the editor's false claims win. Check each claim against the code, because
   a writer and a lead both wrote a false "only reads" in this method's first run.
4. **One more cold read** of the merged text. Report what they said, and label the persona as a simulation.
5. **Only then the human sees one version**, with the alternatives a line each.

## When critics disagree

The editor wins on truth and on the thread. The reader wins on where attention stopped. The human wins on taste, always:
they threw out a subject the simulated reader loved. If the human's reason is new, it goes in the brief and the skill.

## Cost

Keep it proportional: a full round for a piece that reaches customers, a single cold editor for a small change, nothing
for a word the human already chose.
