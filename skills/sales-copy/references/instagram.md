# What only Instagram asks: caption, stories, comments, profile

Read this when a piece goes to Instagram. The project's channel rules (links, labels, which profile talks to whom) win
over this file; this holds what the platform itself rewards.

## Caption: first decide its job

- **Job A, the video already hooked them.** The caption does not hook again, which would compete with the video. It
  gives the context that makes the ask make sense, the words people search, and the ask.
- **Job B, the caption is the content** (a photo, a carousel cover). Line one is the hook and works like a video hook:
  concrete, short, cut at a cliff, never mid-clause.

Say which job before writing.

- **About 125 characters show before "mais".** Write that box first and read it alone. Never a greeting, a hashtag or
  an emoji as the first character there.
- **Search reads the caption.** The phrase a seller would type ("venda no prejuízo", "etiqueta da Shopee") goes in a
  normal sentence of the body. It counts more than a hashtag.
- **Hashtags are labels, not reach: five at most**, specific, on their own line. `#viral`, `#fyp`, `#explorepage` say
  nothing.
- **One ask.** Two asks are none.
- **A keyword ask needs one word people can type**, no spaces or emoji, said in the video too ("comenta ETIQUETA"). It
  only works when something really goes out by DM to whoever comments.
- **No URL in the caption**: it is not clickable. The link goes in the bio, the story sticker or the DM.
- Short wins: a caption that earns the tap and delivers 600 characters beats one with 1,800.

## Stories: depth, not reach

Only followers see them; they decide whether the brand is worth buying from.

- **3 to 7 frames a day**: an opening with something happening, the content, **the ask at frame 4** (one sticker), and
  a close. Past ~7, the last frames play to nobody, which is where people usually put the ask.
- **Stickers by job:** poll for cheap volume, question box to harvest the audience's exact words (every answer is a
  future hook, formula 16), quiz for a common misconception, link only when there is somewhere real to go, countdown for
  a real date.
- **A repost of the feed post with no comment is the most skipped frame on the platform.** Say what happened and why it
  is worth going back.
- **The DM funnel runs one way: they act first, the brand replies.** A story naming a problem, a sticker that lets people
  say "sou eu", then a reply to each. Bulk DMs to people who did not interact get the account restricted.

## Comments and replies

- **Under the brand's own post, reply in the first hour**, in this order: whoever used the keyword, whoever describes the
  problem the product solves (answer fully in public, one line of door at the end), whoever adds or disagrees, then the
  rest. A question many people share becomes a video reply, not a comment.
- **Under other accounts' posts, never pitch.** One to three sentences that add a fact, the missing case or a real
  question; never "ótimo post". A comment that could sit under any post is noise.
- To a critic, concede the true part in their words first, then hold the line. To a hater, nothing.

## Profile: a decision screen

A visitor arrives from one video and gives the profile about three seconds: is there more of that here, and is it for
me.

- **The name field** (the bold line, not the handle) is what search matches: "{Nome} | {o que faz, nas palavras que se
  buscam}".
- **Bio line one says who it is for and what changes**; the rest is one proof or one plain offer.
- **Pin three posts with three jobs**: the best proof, the clearest explanation of the offer, an introduction.
- **Highlights named for a buyer's questions** (Preço, Como funciona, Resultados), never "Aleatório".
- **One link** that matches what the bio promised; a menu converts worse than a door.
- **Grid covers are chosen**, with up to four words, readable at thumbnail size.
