# What already works: the niche, the paid ads and the brand's own posts

Read this before writing a video or an ad with no evidence of which shape works for this audience, and after a few posts
have run, to decide what to make more of.

## The one idea: rank by the outlier multiple

Raw views are not evidence. Views divided by **that account's own median of its recent posts** is: above 3× is a signal
worth copying, below 1.5× is that account's normal day. A big account doing big numbers on a quiet day teaches nothing;
a small account that tripled its own median found something.

Compare against accounts within about 10× the brand's size. A shape that works at two million followers often works
because of the two million.

## Reading the niche

1. **Pick 6 to 12 accounts with the human:** four in the same niche and audience, four adjacent (another niche, the same
   buyer, where formats arrive first), two to four much larger ones for format only. Ask the human to open his own
   saved collection too: it is the corpus already filtered by his taste.
2. **Read, never scrape.** A dozen posts per account, at human speed, in a browser where the human is already logged
   in. Never log in for him, never ask for a password, never run a crawler or a scraping service: automated collection
   breaks the platform's terms and blocks accounts. YouTube Shorts shows views and auto-captions without login, so it is
   a legitimate second corpus for the spoken hook.
3. **Capture per post:** account, followers, the account's median of its last ~12 posts, this post's views, the hook
   verbatim (spoken or on screen, bad grammar included), length and the ask.
4. **Copy the formula, never the video.** The shape of the hook, the structure, the length, the cut pattern. Never the
   script, the voice or the edit, and every row names its source.

## Reading the paid ads

For a paid campaign, also read the platform's public ad library (Meta Ad Library, by page or keyword, country Brasil).
An ad still running after weeks is usually one that pays; a burst of variations of one ad shows which hook the
advertiser is testing. Capture the hook of the first three seconds and how long it has run.

## Saying what it means, carefully

Report three things and no more: which formulas over-index in the top third (with counts), what the top third share
structurally that the bottom does not (hook length, moving first frame, where the ask sits), and the hooks no formula
names, which are either noise or a shape worth adding to `hooks.md`. Then state the sample: forty posts across six
accounts supports a claim, twelve does not.

Hand back hook lines the brand could say tomorrow, never "faz um vídeo igual a esse".

## Auditing the brand's own posts

The brand's own last posts are the only evidence of what works for it; everything else is a prior.

| Metric | How | What it tells |
|---|---|---|
| outlier multiple | views / the profile's median | a real hit or a normal day |
| non-follower reach | share of reach from non-followers | whether it travelled |
| hold at 3 s | still watching at 3 s / started | the hook's grade |
| average watch time | from insights | whether the middle held |
| sends per reach | shares / reach | the strongest signal a post earns |
| follows per reach | follows / reach | whether the profile converted the attention |

- **Views without follows is a profile problem, no views is a hook problem.** Separate the two before rewriting anything.
- Check day and hour last and only if nothing else explains it: it almost never does.
- Six posts do not show a pattern; say so instead of inventing one.
