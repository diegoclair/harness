# Legal sources and how to verify them

Nothing here is a citation to copy. It is a map of where each kind of rule lives and what was checked
in the original case (verified at the source in October 2026). Law and regulation move; open each
provision again before using it and record the date you read it.

## The verification rule

For every provision the petition cites, answer four questions from the source itself:

1. Does it exist with that number?
2. Does it say what the petition attributes to it?
3. If quoted, is the quotation literal and uncut (mark cuts with "(...)")?
4. Was it in force on the date of the fact it is applied to?

A fetch tool that returns a summary has not answered question 3. Ask again for the literal text of the
specific articles, and if two calls disagree, do not quote.

## Primary sources

| Kind of rule | Where |
|---|---|
| Federal statutes (consumer code, small-claims law, civil procedure, civil code, elderly statute, sector framework laws) | planalto.gov.br, compiled text |
| Regulator resolutions | The regulator's own legislation portal. Long pages need paginated reads; the annex with the actual regulation is often in the second half |
| Superior-court theses | The court's own site (ruling text and official news), or another court's official jurisprudence digest quoting it |
| State appellate rulings | The court's jurisprudence search (captcha; see `jurisprudence-search.md`) |
| Small-claims forum statements (enunciados) | The national judiciary council's page |
| Minimum wage (for the cap) | The decree, via official or mainstream news of the decree |

Blogs, law-firm articles and petition templates are leads, never sources.

## What is usually relevant in a consumer small-claims case

Look these up in the consumer code and confirm each: the definitions of consumer and supplier; the
victim-of-the-event equivalence (useful for a co-plaintiff who is a user, not the holder); the basic
rights to clear information, to compensation, and to shifting the burden of proof; strict liability
for defective service; the adequacy-and-continuity duty for essential services (its wording targets
public bodies and their concessionaires — applying it to a privately-authorised service is common but
attackable, so pair it with the sector framework law's user-rights article); the offer-information
duty; abusive practices (excessive advantage; price increase without cause); double restitution of
undue charges and its "justifiable mistake" exception; the registry of substantiated complaints;
interpretation in favour of the consumer; abusive clauses; highlighted drafting of clauses that
restrict rights; consumer's domicile as forum.

In the small-claims law: the cap and the lower cap for acting without a lawyer; forum rules; **the
joinder rule, which requires the cumulated requests to be connected**; witnesses (how many, appearing
without summons); dismissal when the plaintiff misses a hearing.

In the civil procedure code: urgent relief requirements; the provision that relief aimed at inhibiting
the repetition of an unlawful act does not require showing damage (decisive when the service is
working again on the filing date); coercive daily fines; priority handling.

## Sector regulation: the telecom example

The telecom regulator's consumer-rights regulation was replaced: the older one governed facts until
the new one entered into force, and the new one's entry into force was itself postponed. Check which
applied on each date.

Provisions that mattered, to be re-read at the source:

- Suspension for non-payment only after a set number of days from **notifying** the consumer, and what
  the notice must contain.
- A disputed charge **suspends the countdown** to suspension until the provider answers, within a
  deadline.
- During suspension, mobile and fixed-line providers must still guarantee access to the provider's own
  phone support.
- Refund of undue charges in double with correction and interest (the regulation repeats the consumer
  code).
- Minimum advance notice for the end of an offer.
- Every contact gets a protocol, informed at the start.
- In the older regulation: the loyalty contract as a separate instrument, the information that must be
  given before contracting, and the fine tied to **rescission** — which opened the argument that a plan
  downgrade is not a rescission.

The defendant's own invoices may print the rule ("after N days from notification, suspension may
occur"). Quote the invoice: it is the defendant acknowledging the duty.

## Superior-court theses

Two things about a thesis must be read in the ruling, not in a summary:

- **The literal thesis.**
- **The modulation of effects** — from when it applies and to which contracts. The double-restitution
  thesis (bad faith not required when the charge violates objective good faith) applies to amounts
  **paid** after the ruling's publication, in consumer contracts not involving public services. State
  the payment date next to the citation.

A pending repetitive-appeal theme on the same subject is worth knowing about (for example, whether a
prior out-of-court attempt is a precondition to sue). Do not cite a pending theme as law; do make sure
the facts section shows the attempts.

## Traps that were hit

- **Annulled provisions.** A later ruling of the regulator annulled several provisions of its own new
  regulation, including the one that would have limited forced migration to plans of equal or lower
  price. The page shows the text with a note; a summariser may drop the note. Ask explicitly whether
  each provision was annulled or amended.
- **Truncated quotations.** Ending a quote before the sentence ends, without "(...)", was flagged.
- **An agency opinion is not a finding.** "The consumer points out that he was not warned, a situation
  not contested by the supplier" records an allegation and the lack of rebuttal. Quote it whole.
- **A re-sent document is not a second decision.** Compare dates and signatures.
- **Different legal entity on the tax note.** A group may issue part of the invoice through another
  company. Show that the single bill was paid to the defendant.
- **Joinder.** An older claim with a different cause needs an explicit connection paragraph (same
  contract, same line, the older suspension being part of the moral-damages cause as repetition), and a
  fallback: if the judge objects, withdraw that request alone and refile before the limitation date.

## Limits and deadlines to compute

- The cap for acting without a lawyer, at the current minimum wage. Leave room for correction.
- Limitation for restitution: there are competing readings (three, five or ten years). Compute the date
  under each from the **payment** date, say which the petition relies on, and note the most
  conservative date as the practical deadline for any refiling.
- Whether double restitution is realistic: with a signed clause the court tends to see a justifiable
  mistake; ask double and, subsidiarily, simple.
