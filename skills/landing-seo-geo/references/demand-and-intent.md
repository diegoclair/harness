# Demand and intent: measure before choosing a page, a word or a title

A page, a title or a new term is a bet on what people type. Placed without measuring, it targets the
wrong audience (a seller looking for an accountant on an accountant's page) or a query whose intent
is to buy something else. Measure first; every finding is marked measured (source, date) or inferred.

## What to read, in this order

1. **The site's own numbers first** (Search Console, the owner's account). Per page: impressions,
   clicks, CTR, average position, top queries; then the query filter "contains <term>" for each term
   you are about to put in a title. Ask the owner for these one step at a time, saying which button.
   A term with zero impressions on a page that ranks for its neighbours is evidence, not noise.
2. **Relative demand: Google Trends** (public API, country and language set, 12 months and the last
   8 weeks). Compare each candidate against an anchor the reader knows ("how to sell on <marketplace>")
   so a relative index becomes a scale. Trends gives ratios, never volume.
3. **Intent: the autocomplete** (`suggestqueries`, language and country set). What completes a query
   tells who types it: "<assistant> <marketplace>" completing with "subscription/coupon" is a
   purchase search, not a how-to; "how to sell on <channel>" completing with "as an affiliate" is a
   different audience. Read intent before ranking a cluster by size.
4. **Who owns the results** for the biggest queries: format that wins (official doc, directory,
   tool page, long guide, video) and whether anyone answers in the reader's language. Automated
   reading of Google's results page is usually blocked and Bing over `curl` returns degraded robot
   results: neither counts as measurement. Use a search tool's index as a stand-in and mark it
   to-check, or ask the owner for one screenshot of an incognito search.
5. **Absolute volume** only from Keyword Planner (the owner's account): hand them the exact list.

## How to read it

- **Rank clusters by size × intent × our ability to win**, not size alone. The biggest term with the
  wrong intent is worth less than a small one the site already ranks for and that is growing.
- **Separate audiences that share words.** The same term can be typed by the customer and by the
  person serving the customer; write which one each query belongs to and target only yours.
- **A page needs something built to point at.** Demand for "<feature> for <channel>" does not justify
  a page about a feature the product does not deliver; record the demand and wait.
- **A query owned by an official page** (the platform's own help, its app store) is entered through
  the platform's listing, not a competing article.

## How to brief the researcher

A brief that opens with "this page already ranks, don't lose it" gets a conservative answer that
preserves and stops. Give the constraints (what must not change) and the question (how big the
opportunity is, who owns it, what it would take to lead) as separate items, so the protection limits
the plan without shrinking the research.
