# Trust with no customers yet: the founder note

Research round 19 Sep 2026 (pages opened live): Fathom (founder photo + stance quote), Plausible (signed
co-founder statement with concrete facts), Nomad List (a byline linking to the founder's track record),
Baremetrics (verifiable open metrics). Contrast: Buffer, Userlist and Gumroad show no founder once they
have scale or testimonials. No well-known Brazilian SaaS was found with a founder note on the homepage
(only "About" pages) — unverified gap, not a pattern.

## What works
- **Signed note**, 80–150 words, first person, low on the page (after the pitch, before the close).
- Paragraph 1: the **real trigger story** (who, what pain, concretely). Paragraph 2: what the founder found
  in the market *as personal experience* ("I didn't find one that…", never "none exists"), plus one line of
  relevant background stated as a fact, not a boast. Paragraph 3: how the product answers it (only built
  work) and the mission as an ambition, with a commitment ("we work on it every day").
- **Layout that was approved**: small lowercase label ("who makes X"); desktop two columns in the page
  container — large round photo (~240px, ≥2x pixels, candid, head and shoulders, a subtle accent ring) with
  a LinkedIn icon under it; right column: name at heading size, role in the accent, the note between large
  accent quotation marks, ~20px text filling the column. Mobile: stacked, photo ~160px. Its own section
  with standard padding (not squeezed against the FAQ or the close).

## Avoid
- A CV line (languages, stack, companies) — it speaks to recruiters, not customers.
- "The hardest part is X" or any superlative that wasn't measured; say "it started with these pains".
- Slogan closings ("the path is one"), vague mission ("passionate about revolutionising").
- Studio/stock photos, third-person bios, the founder in the hero, invented traction.
