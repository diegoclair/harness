# Writing the spoken script

Read this while writing or changing what a video says.

## Write it as speech

- **The TTS reads the text verbatim.** Write the transcript a person would say: contractions, the rhythm of the
  spoken language, numbers spelled the way they are said. Page text becomes a reading voice.
- **Punctuation is direction.** A comma is a breath, an ellipsis a hesitation, a question mark a rising voice — so a
  statement must never end in "?" unless the voice should ask it.
- **Phonetic spelling only where the chosen voice got a word wrong by ear.** A spelling tuned for one voice harms
  another; keep it scoped to that voice, and keep the caption in the correct spelling.
- **Say a term the way the audience says it**, not the way the product's UI shortens it ("QR code", not "QR").

## Every line earns its place

- **A short ad line is a claim with impact, and a true one.** "Your phone can become a reader" lands harder than
  "your phone already does this" — and the second reads as false when competitors cannot do it.
- **No pointing word repeated across adjacent lines** ("look at this… already does this"): the ear catches it
  before the eye.
- **No promise beyond what the scene proves**, under the project's message rules.
- **Weak glue words go first.** "Aí", "então", "agora" at the start of a line sound like filler or get misread;
  start on the action or the pain.

## Read each variant whole

A hook test shares one body under several hooks. Before any voice exists, read every variant from its own hook to
its close and ask of each line: does it follow from what this variant has already said? A body line that leans on
the pain ("then you have to convert it…") breaks under a hook that never named the pain; that variant needs its own
bridge line, written for it.
